I designed and built this console table as a long, light surface for the window, with enough storage underneath to keep clutter out of sight. It stands 32 inches tall and spans 4 feet across the top, with three soft-close drawers and an open shelf below.

## Design

I wanted the table to feel mid-century, like a vintage Broyhill piece. The detail that carries that look is the scalloped drawer pulls: instead of hardware, each drawer front has a curved, beveled cutout along its edge that works as a finger pull. The three curves read as one continuous wave across the front of the table.

The frame is red oak with poplar legs, and the top, bottom shelf, and drawer fronts are red oak plywood. Everything is glued and screwed together, and the drawers run on soft-close slides.

The scalloped pulls took the most planning. I drew the curve on cardboard to make a template, cut it out, and traced it onto each drawer front so all three would match. Then I cut the curves with a jigsaw tilted to a 30° bevel, which gives each pull its angled edge and makes it easy to grip from above.

To finish, I used a white stain to soften the red tones of the oak and bring the different woods closer together, then sealed everything with clear polyurethane so it can handle plants and daily use.
